Overview

Nocturne Festival

Nocturne is a three-night festival for experimental music, and its identity had to work as loudly at 3 metres as it did at 30 centimetres. The answer was constraint: a single six-column grid, a single grotesque, and a rule that every poster must break exactly one of the system’s rules.

Each night produced four variations — halftone performance photography, duotone colour fields, pure typographic compositions and one deliberate failure per set. The series was screen-printed in two inks, so every decision had to survive a physical process with no undo button.

A poster should stop you at ten metres and reward you at ten centimetres.
Creative process

Four moves, every project

  1. 01

    Grid first

    One six-column grid, two margins, zero exceptions — defined before a single image was chosen.

  2. 02

    Type scale

    A single grotesque pushed from 8pt credits to 400pt headlines, tracking tightened as size grew.

  3. 03

    Image treatment

    Performance photography pushed through halftone, duotone and high-contrast thresholds.

  4. 04

    Print & output

    Two-ink screen printing, paper tests and registration marks as part of the visual language.

How it actually happened

Twelve dummies were printed on newsprint before the final run. Three compositions died on the wall — too clever at distance — and were rebuilt the same night.
